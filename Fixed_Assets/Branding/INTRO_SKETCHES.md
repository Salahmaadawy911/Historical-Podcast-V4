# Intro sketches — the studies that clear

Eight charcoal fragments for the extended `BRAND_bumper_in`. They fade up across the paper,
overlap, build density — and then clear completely as the mark arrives. **The studies go away and
the instrument remains.**

## Why these eight and not historical figures

Sketching recognisable people in the intro makes a promise about who appears on the show, and this
is a fixed asset — that promise would be made in every episode forever, including the ones that go
nowhere near those faces.

So every fragment here is **evidence rather than cast**: the things a historian actually looks at.
They are also deliberately **era-neutral**, because the guest list runs from Cleopatra to
Montezuma, and a colonnade or a trireme would quietly narrow the show to the Mediterranean. A
worn step, a rope, a plain pot and a coastline belong to everybody.

They are also chosen for **eight different silhouettes** — wide, tall, small and round, branching,
low and horizontal. That matters more than subject once they are overlapping on a page: eight
similar shapes read as wallpaper.

## Rules for all eight

- **Square format.** One object per image, nothing else in frame.
- **Paste each prompt as one unbroken line.**
- **Reject anything with a face, a figure, a background scene or a horizon line.** The object sits
  on bare paper — no setting, no ground plane, no sky.
- **Reject anything that looks finished.** These are working studies. If one arrives looking like
  a gallery drawing it will fight the others and read as decoration.
- Generate in the usual charcoal style — roughly 3 credits each, ~24 for the set, one time forever.

## The act-break studies — **CHOSEN 2026-09-18**

`BRAND_actbreak` uses **two** studies, alternated, and they are deliberately **not** the opening's
three. Hills, the doorway and the hand appear thirty seconds into every episode; meeting one of
them again at a break reads as running out of material rather than as a motif. Breaks own their own
subjects so the two registers stay apart. Spec for the break itself is in `SERIES_FURNITURE.md`.

**Generated at 4 seconds, not 3.** The clip length *is* the break length — the whole point is that
nothing is edited — so the draw has to fill 0.000 to 4.000. Standard, audio off, 32 cr a clip.

⚠️ **A 4s clip gives the model more room to wander than a 3s one** — the creeping zoom that the
camera-stationary preset exists to stop. Keep the preset on and reject any drift; this is the one
respect in which these are riskier than the intro clips.

The draw-on test subject. It came back clean first time, with a real light direction and a cast
shadow on the page that grounds it without inventing a floor — which is exactly what these need.
**Use the Vessel prompt above verbatim.** It is proven, and a proven prompt is not improved by
editing it. Only the clip is regenerated, at 4s instead of 3.

### Break B — **the worn step is dropped.** Tested before this file existed and it did not come back well

Reported 2026-09-18: the step had already been generated at some earlier point and the results were
poor. That is the second time a subject on the reserve list has failed in a way the list did not
record, so it is written down here now: **the step is dropped, not deferred.**

The strengthened prompt that briefly stood here — naming the light direction, describing the hollow
as a pool of shade — is kept in git history but not re-run. It addressed a real fault, but the
subject had already been tried, and a rewrite is worth one reroll on an untested subject, not on one

**What survives is the idea, not the noun.** The step was on the list because *wear* is what it
draws: a surface shaped by use over a long time, which is exactly what an act break is about. Two
replacements below keep that and drop the architecture.

## The draw-on video — one prompt for all three

**Kling, image-to-video with both frames set.** Start frame `paper_source.png`, end frame that
study. **3 seconds, Standard, audio off** (8 cr/s — nothing here makes a sound, and the bumper's
audio is already built), with the **camera-stationary preset on**. Same mechanism as the outro
transformation, which is the proof it works at all.

**3 seconds, not 5.** The outro is 5 because it is a slow dissolve of a full photographic scene and
needs the room; this is a quick study and does not. Two reasons the shorter clip is actually better:
the first second of a draw-on is a nearly blank page, so 3 seconds yields about 2 seconds of usable
build, which is close to what each study gets on screen once they are overlapped a second apart —
almost no speed-up needed, where a 5-second clip would have to run at double speed. And **the longer
the clip, the more room the model has to wander**: a creeping zoom, a drifting page, the object
starting to breathe. A first 5-second test came back with exactly that small zoom, which is what the
preset now exists to stop.

Nothing in the prompt names the object, so it is identical for the delta, the doorway and the hand.

```
Charcoal marks appear on the blank page and accumulate into the finished study. They arrive in patches rather than all at once — one area of the drawing darkens and completes, then the next begins, the broad tonal masses first and the finer smudged shading after. The drawing is complete a moment before the clip ends and then holds still. The page is still and the camera is still: no pan, no zoom, no drift, no shake, no page turn. Nothing in the picture is animated except the marks arriving — the object does not move, shift, breathe or come to life, and no hand, pencil, brush or tool is visible anywhere in the frame. It stays a drawing for every frame of the clip; the tone and the paper grain remain visible throughout, and it never resolves into photographic footage.
```

**Three things that prompt is doing, all of them learned the hard way:**

- **"In patches rather than all at once."** The predicted failure is the model dissolving the whole
  image up evenly, which reads as a crossfade rather than drawing. Describing *how* marks arrive is
  the counter — and per the Mode 4 rule, the construction is described rather than the fault named,
  because naming a fault puts it in the prompt.
- **"No hand, pencil, brush or tool is visible."** Left open, the model will often add a drawing
  hand. That is a much more literal idea than this intro wants, and it would have to be consistent
  across all three clips, which it will not be.
- **"The camera is still."** The bumper already carries a slow push on the paper. A second move
  inside the clip fights it.

**Reject a take if:** the whole image fades up evenly, the object moves, a hand or pencil appears,
the camera drifts, or the page turns. **Keep the raw clips** — the build trims each one to its
active stretch, and the first second of a draw-on is a nearly blank page.

### The model changes the paper — fix it after, not in the prompt

**Reported and measured 2026-09-18.** Across many generations the recurring fault was never the
subject; it was that the drawing came back on a page that is not quite `paper_source.png`. No amount
of prompt wording fixed it, and the reason is structural: the model is *redrawing* the page it was
shown, not compositing onto it, so some difference is guaranteed.

**Measured on the two 4s break clips:**

| | R | G | B |
|---|---|---|---|
| `paper_source.png` margin | 204.7 | 195.8 | 179.9 |
| clip frame 0 margin | 202.7 | 194.2 | 178.1 |
| clip frame 96, gain vs frame 0 | ×0.988 | ×0.985 | ×0.991 |

So the page starts about **1% dark** and loses a further **1.0–1.5%** across four seconds. In
isolation that is invisible — which is why it survived so many rerolls. Cut against the opening,
which uses the real page, it is a tone step on the edit.

**The fix is arithmetic and it is exact.** A drawing on paper is multiplicative: what you see is
paper × ink. So:

1. Measure each frame's paper gain in the **outer 5% margin**, where no drawing ever reaches, and
   divide it out. That removes the drift exactly, frame by frame.
2. Divide the frame by **its own frame 0** to get ink transmittance alone.
3. Multiply by the real `paper_source.png`.

The clip's own first frame is the blank page, because the start frame *is* `paper_source.png`, so
nothing has to be tone-matched by hand and whatever paper the model invented is discarded entirely.
`Fixed_Assets/Branding/intro_source/paper_restore.py` does this; run it on **every** draw-on clip
before the clip becomes an asset.

⚠️ **Stop rerolling for paper.** Judge a take on the drawing — subject, light direction, tone, no
hand, no camera drift — and let the build put the page back. Rerolling for paper colour is chasing
something the prompt cannot control and the build fixes for nothing.

### Do not pass the paper as a reference image

It is the obvious thing to try and it makes the result worse. If the model is shown the paper it
will reproduce the paper, and compositing that over the real page stacks **two grain patterns** —
which reads as a photocopy of a drawing lying on a desk, rather than a drawing made on the desk.

What the composite needs from each file is the **marks only**. Each sketch gets flat-field
normalised so its own paper goes to white, then lands with a darken blend, so the page underneath
stays the actual page the mark appears on. Same correction already used to build `paper_source.png`
— it preserves the marks' contrast because it scales tone ratios rather than clipping them.

So the prompts stand as written, and the thing to check on arrival is **tonal contrast**, not paper
colour. A drawing whose darkest tone is mid-grey will normalise to something faint and washed out.

**The strikes land at 0, 4, 8 and 12 — dead regular across the join.** That is the thing that
makes the hook and the bumper read as one piece rather than two. The slot is one loop unit plus
the run-in, so the clock never breaks stride.

**The slot is 6.17 seconds, and that is one line — not a cold open.** Strike, line, strike. The
second strike closes the sentence, which a cut cannot do. A line that will not land in four seconds
is probably not the shocking one. If one ever needs more air, 10.15s gives it two gaps.

Front matter totals about **22 seconds** — 3s disclosure card, 6.17s hook, 13s bumper.

**Full frame, not scaled and shared.** The first build placed all three studies on one page at
35–40% and it read as incidental; composited at full size on top of each other it was a single dark
smear. Full frame and alone, each reads as the drawing someone is actually making.

**No erase pass.** Drawing each study then running it backwards to rub it out costs exactly as long
as drawing did, for the half where nothing new happens — it would push this past 23 seconds. The
0.4s dissolve does the same job.

⚠️ **Four subjects failed, all for one reason: this style cannot draw lines.** The block says
*forms built from tone rather than outline* and *no outlining*, so any subject whose character is
linear is asking for two incompatible things. That killed the **delta** and **coastline** (aerial
patterns, no light direction, no form), the **tree** (branches are lines) and the **growth rings**
(concentric lines). A **stone** generated but was inert — form with no information.

**The set is hills, doorway, hand.** The hills bleed to the frame edge where the others float on
paper with margins — a real inconsistency, accepted because it costs less than more rerolls. If it
is ever revisited, a **large shell** is the best untried candidate: solid, curved, tonal, and its
spiral is a form rather than a line.
