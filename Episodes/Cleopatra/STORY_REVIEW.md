## 2 · Lines that do nothing — candidates to cut

- **P2 B `OFFMIC` "Everything."** An echo and nothing more. Cutting it costs nothing.
- **P1 B `OFFMIC` "In his house."** Also an echo, but it sets up the `BEAT` and *"Did you watch?"*. Keep
  it unless Part 1 runs long.
- **P2 D "Plutarch says he was angry…" / "Angry that he lost his procession…"** (7:24–7:35). True and
  dry, but the act already has its turn; this is the likeliest 11 s to lose if Part 2 is tightened
  rather than lengthened.
- **P2 B "the Partners in Death" exchange** (2:36–2:49). Good colour, but it and the Caesarion-enrolled
  exchange both run before the Red Sea: three small beats before the act's real question (Pelusium).
  See §4.

## 3 · Moments a newcomer would not follow

- **"talents"** (P1 A, 2:12). No gloss, and the number means nothing without one. Either a context card
  (WHAT · talent) or let the `OFFMIC` *"Charming."* carry it as tone. **My recommendation is a card**,
  written in Mode 4 from a sourced weight.
- **"censor"** (P1 A, 2:25). This one reads fine: "a Roman official" is all anyone needs, and the line
  explains what he tried.
- **Two Caesars.** Octavian is called Octavian throughout. The one place the double name matters (*"a
  Caesar too many"*) now has the host say *"Octavian called himself Caesar too"* (Suetonius *Aug.* 7).
- **"Seleucus" and "the commander"** (P2 B). The host names him once and then calls him the commander.
  That's clear.
- **P1 Act B → C.** No act break separates Caesar's death from Tarsus. The *"Who did you back?" /
  "Whoever was going to win"* exchange carries the three-year jump, and a viewer will follow it.

## 4 · Where a viewer is most likely to leave

1. **P1, 2:21–2:50.** The annexation attempt, then the father's will: two background exchanges back to
   back, the only stretch of Part 1 without a turn or an image. It is short, and the Pompey story lands
   right after it. If Part 1 is tightened, the censor exchange is the one to cut.
2. **P2, 2:15–3:27.** Four small beats (terms, dining club, Caesarion enrolled, Red Sea) before
   Pelusium. Each is sourced and each moves, but the act is episodic here. The Red Sea image is the
   strongest of them, so the dining club is the one to go.
3. **P2, the close (8:51–9:19)** is short and ends on her. Nothing after the last line, so no goodbye to
   leave on.

## 5 · The two arc checks

**Does Part 1 stand alone? Yes.**
- The charge is put in the first 15 seconds.
- Her answer arrives as a thesis at 8:43 (*"Rome calls that seduction. It is what a kingdom without an
  army does"*).
- It ends on the one question the record leaves open (*"Did you run?"*) as an invitation, not a
  withheld ending.
- Someone who never watches Part 2 has still heard the whole argument.

**Does Part 2 pay exactly what Part 1 promised? Yes.**
- Part 1 promised *"next time, you tell it from yours"* about Actium. Part 2's first question is *"Did
  you run?"*, and she answers it at once.
- The deeper promise, the charge itself, is paid by the end. She decided her own death, her own burial
  and her own last word, while losing everything else.
- Part 2 also re-establishes the stakes in one direct-address exchange for anyone arriving cold.

**Where Part 1 breaks.** At Actium's flight: the strongest unanswered question in the record. This
coincides with where v1 broke. It was chosen by the rule, and the close's words, hook and hand-off are
new.

## 6 · Cold-reader pass — read as someone who knows none of it

- **The opening works cold.** "Rome declared war on a woman" → "Seductress. Monster." → the woman in
  the chair. The hook (*"They came for my treasury. They wrote about my bed."*) makes sense without any
  context.
- **Pompey's head is the first moment a newcomer leans in** (2:54). It arrives about three minutes in,
  which is late-ish, but the beauty and Macedonian reversals before it keep the turns coming.
- **The Arsinoe crack depends on the viewer remembering Act B's triumph.** It is 1:40 earlier, and the
  host says her name again before the line, which is enough.
- **Part 2's hook is dark out of context** (*"I gave him the name that killed him."*). A cold viewer
  won't know who "him" is. That is the point, and it pays at 8:45. If it reads too bleak for the first
  four seconds, a lighter alternative from the same part is *"Nobody carries sails into a battle he
  means to stay in."*, though it runs longer than ten syllables.
- **The death is discussed, never shown.** No method is described beyond the sources' uncertainty. The
  poison tests Plutarch reports (*Ant.* 71) are **deliberately left out**, because they would put a
  method on screen. The b-roll carries no snake, no body and no death.
